01Generative QML & mutual information

Mutual information as a training objective for quantum generators

Most quantum generative models are trained against fidelity or a Wasserstein-style discriminator. Both have known failure modes — fidelity collapses on highly mixed targets, and the Wasserstein critic inherits all the instabilities of its classical counterpart on near-term hardware.

An alternative is to maximize the mutual information between a latent quantum register and the generated state. The objective is symmetric in the two registers, well-defined for mixed targets, and admits practical lower bounds via the disentangling QNN we developed in earlier work. The Sci. Reports paper shows this works on toy distributions and on financial return series.

02Entropy & distance estimation

One disentangling QNN, several quantities

Estimating quantum entropies, fidelities, and trace distances is usually done with a different protocol per quantity — SWAP test, Hadamard test, quantum singular-value transformations. Each requires a fresh circuit and a fresh sampling complexity bound.

Our PRA paper shows that a disentangling ansatz, trained once, gives unified estimators for several of these quantities. The paper's contribution is more architectural than algorithmic: it gives a clean primitive that downstream work — including the QMI-GAN above — can call as a black box.

Papers

03Hardware-aware variational algorithms

Hardware-aware variational eigensolvers

Reported VQE numbers are usually iteration-bound. That hides where the real differences live: gradient evaluation cost, transpilation, classical optimizer overhead, hardware-noise stochasticity. A method that converges in fewer iterations is not always the better one when the clock is running.

AutoVQE is a small, deliberately under-engineered harness that fixes the wall-clock at 2^(n-2) seconds, leaves a single editable train.py, and asks which design choices actually matter at that budget. Early experiments on small molecules and the transverse-field Ising model.

04Quantum-inspired finance

What MI-based feature screening buys an alpha researcher

Two of the experiments in the QMI-GAN paper are financial. That is not a coincidence: the same lower-bound machinery used to train the generator also gives a usable, monotonic signal for screening features against a target return series.

The Quant Alpha Dashboard is the ongoing receipt of that — a working dashboard for factor IC, regime tagging, and an MI screener that pretends to be nothing fancier than a sorted table.
